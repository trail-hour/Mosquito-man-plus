Every spring, as temperatures climb and the snow finally clears from backyards across Durham Region, mosquitoes start showing up right on schedule. For homeowners in Oshawa, Whitby, and Ajax, understanding when mosquito season really begins — and why it seems to last longer some years than others — makes it a lot easier to plan ahead for patio dinners, backyard barbecues, and evenings on the deck.

When Does Mosquito Season Start in the GTA?

In most years, mosquito activity across the GTA begins ramping up in late April once daytime temperatures consistently stay above 10°C. Eggs that overwintered in damp soil, leaf litter, or standing water start hatching as soon as conditions warm up. By late May and into June, populations are usually in full swing, and they tend to stay active right through until the first hard frost, which in Durham Region often doesn't arrive until mid-to-late October.

That's a long stretch — five to six months — where mosquitoes can realistically be a nuisance in your yard. Warmer, wetter years tend to push that window even further, while cooler or drier stretches can slow things down temporarily. But overall, homeowners across the region should expect mosquito pressure for most of the frost-free season.

Why Some Durham Region Yards Get Hit Harder

Not every property deals with the same level of mosquito activity, even within the same neighbourhood. A few factors tend to make certain yards more attractive to mosquitoes:

  • Standing water in eavestroughs, plant saucers, birdbaths, or kids' toys left outside
  • Dense shade from mature trees or thick landscaping that keeps humidity high
  • Proximity to ravines, ponds, creeks, or wetlands, which are common throughout Durham Region
  • Tall grass or overgrown garden beds that trap moisture close to the ground

Homes near natural water features in places like Pickering, Whitby, or the rural edges of Clarington often notice mosquitoes arriving a little earlier in the season and sticking around a little later, simply because those areas provide more breeding habitat nearby.

How Weather Patterns Affect GTA Mosquito Activity

Rainfall plays a huge role. A wet spring followed by a humid summer is close to ideal for mosquito breeding, since it keeps low-lying puddles, ditches, and soggy patches of lawn wet for days at a time — long enough for eggs to hatch and larvae to mature. Conversely, a dry stretch in July or August can knock populations back temporarily, though a single heavy rainfall can bring numbers right back up within a week or two.

This is part of why mosquito activity can feel unpredictable from one month to the next. A quiet June doesn't necessarily mean a quiet August, especially in a region as geographically varied as the GTA, where local microclimates near lakes, rivers, and green spaces behave differently than drier inland neighbourhoods.

Peak Months and What to Expect

For most Durham Region homeowners, the heaviest mosquito activity falls between mid-June and late August, when both temperature and humidity are consistently high. Dawn and dusk are typically the worst times of day, since many common mosquito species are most active during those cooler, low-light hours. That said, on overcast or particularly humid days, they can be a problem well into the afternoon too.

September often brings a second smaller wave of activity as temperatures moderate again, before things finally taper off with the first cold nights of fall. Homeowners in Bowmanville, Courtice, and Brooklin frequently notice this late-season uptick catches them off guard after assuming the worst was behind them.

Practical Steps for Homeowners Across the GTA

While no yard can be made completely mosquito-free, there's a lot homeowners can do to reduce numbers noticeably:

  1. Empty or refresh any standing water around the property at least once a week
  2. Keep gutters clear so water doesn't pool after rain
  3. Trim back overgrown shrubs and tall grass to reduce humid hiding spots
  4. Store items that can collect rainwater, like buckets or wheelbarrows, upside down
  5. Consider larger-scale landscaping fixes, such as improving drainage in consistently soggy areas

These habits help, but they usually aren't enough on their own to keep a yard comfortable during peak mosquito months, especially in leafier, shadier neighbourhoods. That's where a more structured approach comes in.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does mosquito season typically start in the GTA?

Mosquito activity usually begins in late April once daytime temperatures stay consistently above 10°C, with populations building through May and June.

How long does mosquito season last in Durham Region?

It generally runs from late April through mid-to-late October, though warm, wet years can extend the season further on both ends.

Why does my yard seem to have more mosquitoes than my neighbour's?

Standing water, dense shade, tall grass, and proximity to ravines or ponds all increase mosquito activity, so even nearby yards can have very different conditions.

Does rain really make mosquito season worse?

Yes, extended wet periods create more breeding sites in puddles, ditches, and soggy lawn areas, which can lead to noticeable increases in activity within one to two weeks.

Is it worth starting mosquito control before summer really begins?

Starting a treatment plan in late spring tends to work better than waiting, since it targets mosquitoes earlier in their life cycle before populations peak in mid-summer.
