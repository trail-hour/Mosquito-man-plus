Tick sightings have been climbing steadily across Durham Region over the past several years, and homeowners from Oshawa to Clarington are noticing more of them in backyards, on trails, and even in well-maintained lawns. Ticks don't need deep forest to survive; they thrive in the tall grass, leaf piles, and shaded brush that border many suburban and semi-rural properties in this part of the GTA. This checklist breaks down the practical steps homeowners can take to reduce tick activity around their property, along with a few reminders about when a professional visit makes sense.

Why Tick Activity Is Rising Across Durham Region and the GTA

Ticks are most active in warm, humid conditions with easy access to shade and moisture. Durham Region's landscape, with its mix of wooded ravines, conservation areas, and green space woven through residential neighbourhoods, gives ticks plenty of places to hide and move between properties. Towns like Whitby and Ajax have seen new subdivisions built right up against wooded lots, and that overlap is exactly where tick populations tend to establish themselves. Yard Maintenance Checklist for Reducing Tick Habitat

Most tick problems start with yard conditions that are easy to fix once you know what to look for. Work through this list section by section:

  • Mow the lawn regularly and keep grass height under three inches, especially near fence lines and property edges.
  • Rake up leaf litter each fall and spring, since damp leaf piles are one of the most common tick habitats in Durham Region yards.
  • Trim back low branches and shrubs to let more sunlight and airflow reach the ground.
  • Create a barrier of wood chips or gravel, roughly three feet wide, between lawns and wooded or brushy areas.
  • Stack firewood neatly and away from the house, ideally in a dry, sunny spot rather than a shaded corner.
  • Keep playground equipment, patios, and seating areas away from yard edges and tree lines.

These steps won't eliminate ticks completely, but they remove the conditions ticks rely on most, which cuts down significantly on how many make it onto your property in the first place.

Protecting Pets and Family Members During Tick Season

Pets are often the first ones to pick up ticks, since they spend more time low to the ground in grass and brush. A regular check routine matters as much as any yard treatment:

  • Check pets after every walk, particularly around the ears, neck, and paws.
  • Talk to your veterinarian about tick prevention products suited to your pet's size and lifestyle.
  • Check kids and adults after yard work, gardening, or hikes, paying close attention to hairlines, behind knees, and underarms.
  • Shower within a couple of hours of coming inside, which can help wash off ticks that haven't attached yet.
  • Put outdoor clothes directly into the dryer on high heat for at least ten minutes to kill any ticks that hitched a ride.

Families in Pickering and Ajax who spend a lot of time near trails or conservation areas should treat these checks as a routine part of coming home, not just something to remember occasionally.

Personal Protection Steps for Hiking and Yard Work

Whether you're working in the garden or heading out on a trail near Port Perry or Uxbridge, a few simple habits reduce your chances of a tick bite:

  1. Wear light-coloured clothing so ticks are easier to spot before they attach.
  2. Tuck pants into socks when walking through tall grass or brush.
  3. Use an EPA or Health Canada PMRA-registered tick repellent on skin and clothing.
  4. Stick to the centre of trails rather than brushing against tall grass or overhanging branches.
  5. Do a full-body tick check as soon as you're back indoors.

None of these steps take much time, but together they make a real difference, especially during peak tick activity from late spring through early fall.

Building a Seasonal Tick Control Routine

Tick control works best as an ongoing routine rather than a one-time task. A practical seasonal approach looks like this:

  • Early spring: clear leaf litter, trim back overgrowth, and refresh any mulch or gravel barriers.
  • Late spring through summer: keep up with regular mowing and continue daily tick checks for pets and family.
  • Fall: do a final yard cleanup before leaves pile up again, and check outdoor gear before storing it for winter.
  • Year-round: stay alert on warmer winter days, since ticks can become active any time temperatures rise above freezing for an extended stretch.

Whether you're in Whitby, Scarborough, or one of the smaller Durham Region communities, staying consistent with these habits is what keeps tick populations from building up around a property over multiple seasons. Frequently Asked Questions

When does tick season start in Durham Region?

Ticks can become active any time temperatures rise above freezing, but activity typically ramps up in April and stays high through October. Warmer winter days can also bring short bursts of activity outside the usual season.

Are ticks common in Oshawa and Whitby backyards, or mainly in wooded areas?

While wooded and brushy areas have higher tick populations, ticks regularly show up in suburban backyards in Oshawa, Whitby, and neighbouring towns, especially near tree lines, tall grass, and leaf piles.

How can I tell if a tick bite needs medical attention?

If you notice a spreading rash, flu-like symptoms, or the tick was attached for an extended period, it's worth contacting a doctor. Removing the tick promptly and keeping an eye on the bite site for a few weeks is a reasonable first step.

Do professional tick treatments harm pets or pollinators?

Reputable tick control services use EPA and PMRA-registered products applied specifically to problem areas like tree lines and tall grass, minimizing exposure to pets and beneficial insects. It's still a good idea to ask your provider about timing pets' access to treated areas.

How often should I have my yard treated for ticks in Durham Region?

Most seasonal programs involve multiple visits between spring and fall, timed around peak tick activity. Properties with heavy wooded borders, common in areas like Clarington or Scugog, may benefit from more frequent check-ins.
